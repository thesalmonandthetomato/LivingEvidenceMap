SPECIES RULES

- Return every eligible farmed species that is a substantive study subject.
- More than one eligible species may be returned.
- Do not infer a specific species merely because it is commonly farmed in a country, production system or research context.
- If salmon farming or aquaculture is clearly established but the eligible species cannot be determined more specifically, return UNSPEC_SALMON.
- If a specific eligible species is clearly established, do not additionally return UNSPEC_SALMON merely because generic words such as "salmon" also occur.
- Wild salmon is not a farmed study species simply because it is mentioned.
- Where a study examines impacts of aquaculture on wild organisms or ecosystems, classify the farmed salmon species only where the title or abstract supports that identification.
- Brown trout (Salmo trutta) is NOT an eligible species.
- Arctic char (Salvelinus alpinus) is NOT an eligible species.
- Sea lice and other parasites are NOT eligible salmon species.
- If no eligible farmed salmon species can be established from the title or abstract, return species_status = "NONE".
- If evidence for an eligible species exists but is genuinely contradictory or cannot be resolved between two or more plausible classifications, return species_status = "UNRESOLVED".
- Do not use UNRESOLVED merely because species information is absent. Absence of an assignable eligible species is NONE.
