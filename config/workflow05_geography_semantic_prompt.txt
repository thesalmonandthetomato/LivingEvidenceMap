You are extracting substantive study geography from the TITLE and ABSTRACT of a scientific publication for a systematic evidence map of salmon aquaculture.

Use ONLY the supplied title and abstract. Do not use outside knowledge except to map an explicitly stated, unambiguous subnational place to its country.

TASK

Identify every country in which the study itself was substantively conducted.

A country qualifies as substantive study geography only when the title or abstract explicitly indicates that one or more of the following occurred there:
- field sampling or observations;
- aquaculture or farm production, monitoring, husbandry or intervention;
- an experiment or trial conducted at a stated location;
- study animals, farms, facilities, rivers, coastal areas, ecosystems or other study populations located there;
- collection of biological, environmental, social or economic data;
- interviews, surveys or stakeholder research undertaken there;
- a geographically defined case study.

A country may be stated directly or through an unambiguous subnational place. For example, Scotland maps to United Kingdom, Tasmania to Australia, and Galicia to Spain. Do not infer a country from an ambiguous place name.

A country also qualifies when the country, region, industry, policy system, market, fishery, aquaculture sector, production system or geographically defined study material is itself an explicit object of the study, even if no physical sampling or experiment occurred there.

Examples include studies explicitly about:
- Scottish salmon aquaculture;
- Chile's salmon industry;
- Norwegian salmon farms;
- contaminant concentrations in Norwegian and Scottish farmed salmon;
- consumer perceptions of Norwegian salmon in another country.

These examples are illustrative only. Apply the same rule to equivalent substantive geographical framing in any country.

Geographic descriptors such as "Norwegian salmon", "Scottish salmon", or "Chilean salmon" may establish substantive geography when geographic origin, production system, provenance, industry or sector is itself part of the study. Do not assign geography when the descriptor is merely incidental, background, or part of an unrelated comparison.

EXCLUDE INCIDENTAL GEOGRAPHY

Do NOT classify a country merely because it appears in:
- an author affiliation;
- a university, laboratory, institute or research-group name;
- the location of a supplier, manufacturer, commercial company or equipment source;
- a funding body or organisation name;
- publisher or software information;
- background or contextual discussion;
- previous studies or literature comparisons;
- references to policies, regulations or industries elsewhere;
- a species common name, strain name, product name or country adjective that does not locate the study;
- general statements about where aquaculture occurs;
- geographical framing of an environmental or policy concern when the study activity itself is not explicitly located there.

A contextual statement such as "concern about effects in the Bay of Fundy" does NOT establish Canada as study geography unless the title or abstract also states or clearly implies that sampling, experimentation, observation, farm activity, fieldwork, surveys or another substantive study activity occurred there.

Do not infer geography from species, authors, institutional affiliation, language, nationality, production system, or general knowledge.

Do not map historical or obsolete geopolitical entities to a modern country unless the title or abstract itself permits an unambiguous mapping. If a historical entity is substantively part of the study but cannot be mapped confidently from the supplied text, return UNRESOLVED.
