# Results

## Review process

The review process is outlined in the flow diagram in Figure 1. Across
the 5 databases searched, a total of 90,137 search results was obtained.
Following deduplication, 32,292 unique candidate records remained. Title
and abstract repair and enrichment supplemented 2,190 records, whilst a
sweep for retractions identified 9 records that were excluded from
further analysis.

Following screening of titles and abstracts, 19,117 were included in the
map, and passed on to annotation. Species annotations were obtained for
all 19,117 records, geography for 7,618 records, and topic
classifications for 18,886 records.

## Publication year

The volume of academic evidence on salmon farming increased markedly
over time. There were 1,123 included records published in 2025, the
highest complete publication year in the current corpus, compared with
just 475 in 2010.

The three largest focal species categories were Rainbow trout (7,820
records; 40.9%), Atlantic salmon (7,368; 38.5%) and Unspecified species
(3,900; 20.4%). Species categories are not mutually exclusive because
individual records can refer to more than one eligible farmed species.

## Geography

A substantive study country could be resolved for 7,618 records (39.8%),
while 11,499 included records (60.2%) did not contain sufficient
evidence for a defensible country-level assignment. Among records with
assigned geography (Figure 5), the largest country evidence bases were
Norway (1,752 records), Canada (966), Chile (947), United Kingdom (820),
United States (561), Turkey (413). Countries demonstrated strong
species-specificity, as expected (Figure 6), with the major Atlantic
salmon producing countries contributing most to the evidence base, and a
large number of other countries contributing collectively to rainbow
trout research (e.g. Turkey). Records may be assigned to more than one
country where the substantive study spans multiple countries.

## Topics

At least one topic was assigned to 18,886 of the 19,117 included
records; 231 records (1.2%) remained uncoded for topic. Across coded
records, the final ontology contained 53,288 distinct record-topic
assignments.

The evidence base was strongly oriented towards Production, represented
in 13,984 records (73.1%). Environment was the second-largest theme
(4,657; 24.4%), followed by Methods (2,436; 12.7%), Product (2,360;
12.3%) and Industry and governance (2,306; 12.1%). People and society
(936; 4.9%) and Inputs and resources (747; 3.9%) were substantially
smaller. Theme percentages are not mutually exclusive because a record
can be assigned to more than one theme.

### Production

Production research was centred on fish performance and biology, fish
health, feed and nutrition, breeding and reproduction, and stress and
mortality. The most frequently assigned terminal topic was Physiology,
metabolism and biological responses (4,617 records; 24.2%), followed by
Growth and development (2,787; 14.6%). Other major concentrations
included Other diseases - Prevention and treatment (1,756; 9.2%), Other
diseases - General (1,363; 7.1%), Alternative feed ingredients (1,358;
7.1%) and Other diseases - Epidemiology (1,337; 7.0%). Sea lice remained
a substantial research cluster, including 642 records on Sea lice
control and treatment and 504 on Sea lice epidemiology.

### People and society

People and society was much smaller than the Production and Environment
evidence bases. The largest terminal topics were Physiology, metabolism
and biological responses (4,617; 24.2%), Growth and development (2,787;
14.6%) and Other diseases - Prevention and treatment (1,756; 9.2%).
Topics relating to Local and rural development, Livelihoods and income,
Indigenous peoples and rights, Employment and job creation, Food
availability and nutritional security, Culture, identity and traditions,
Working conditions and occupational health, Distribution of benefits and
harms, Local and Indigenous knowledge, Displacement, disruption and
wellbeing, Multiple or general social impacts, Environmental justice and
human rights, Competition with other food uses, Health impacts of
aquaculture, Labour rights, exploitation and migration, and
Affordability and access each represented under 0.5% of the full
evidence map.

## Comparison with secondary research

The supplied manuscript draft compares this evidence map with a recent
umbrella review of 37 systematic reviews across salmon aquaculture.
Recalculation against the final canonical evidence map confirms the
principal imbalance identified in the draft. Sea lice control and
treatment occurs in 642 mapped records (3.4%), compared with 8 of 37
systematic reviews (21.6%) in the umbrella-review dataset. By contrast,
several of the largest areas in the mapped literature have comparatively
little systematic-review coverage, including Physiology, metabolism and
biological responses (24.2% of mapped records), Growth and development
(14.6%), and major disease, environmental and methodological topics.

The social evidence base is particularly weakly represented in the
systematic-review literature described in the draft. In the final
canonical map, Antimicrobial resistance and One Health accounts for
24.2% of records, Conflict and social licence for 14.6%, and Public
discourse and representation for 9.2%; the supplied umbrella-review
dataset contains one review on antimicrobial resistance/One Health and
none in most of the other social categories. Overall, the comparison
continues to indicate that the systematic-review evidence covers a
narrower subset of the topics represented across the mapped literature,
with particularly strong representation of sea lice and limited coverage
of several major physiological, environmental, methodological,
governance and social research areas.
